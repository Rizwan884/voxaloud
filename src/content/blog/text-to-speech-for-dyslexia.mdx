Dyslexia and other learning differences affect how individuals process written language. However, utilizing **text to speech for dyslexia** can bridge the gap by converting static text into clear, spoken audio. In this article, we examine how **tts accessibility** features help readers, and how modern speech engines function alongside a traditional **screen reader** to support learning.

Making text audible helps users follow along easily and improves comprehension.

## 1. How Auditory Learning Aids Comprehension

For readers with dyslexia, matching written characters with their phonetic sounds can be mentally exhausting. Auditory conversion allows the brain to process the content directly without visual strain. This method has been shown to improve vocabulary retention, reading speed, and overall confidence.
